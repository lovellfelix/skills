```
🌟 FEATURED SPOTLIGHT

"Bringing Opportunity Home": Kevin & Maya's Back-to-School Mission

Kevin Williams, 38, moved to Toronto 15 years ago. His sister Maya, 34, 
stayed in Grenada. Together, they're making sure island kids get school 
supplies and a shot at success.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Tell us about the back-to-school initiative. What made you start it?

KEVIN: Growing up in Grand Anse, my family didn't have much. Every 
August, my mom would stretch to buy notebooks and uniforms for me and 
my sister. It was stressful. When I left for Toronto and built a career, 
I realized I could help other families avoid that stress. I called Maya 
and said, "Let's do this together."

That was three years ago. We started small—helping 20 kids in one school. 
Last year, we reached 150 kids across five schools in St. Andrew's and 
Carriacou. This year, we're targeting 200.

What does the program cover?

MAYA: Everything a kid needs for a successful school year:
• Uniforms (two sets, tailored)
• Notebooks, pens, pencils (full pack)
• Shoes (sturdy, proper fit)
• Calculator (for secondary students)
• School bag (durable, local suppliers when possible)

Plus: We provide a "mentorship buddy" (Kevin or another diaspora 
volunteer) who checks in monthly to encourage the kids and help 
with homework if needed.

How do you fund this?

KEVIN: Mostly corporate sponsorships from Grenadian businesses in 
Toronto and diaspora donors. We're transparent about costs:
• Average cost per child: $200 USD
• 200 kids this year: $40,000 budget
• We've raised $26,000 so far; $14,000 to go.

We also get in-kind donations—local companies provide uniforms at cost, 
and schools help identify kids in greatest need.

What impact have you seen?

MAYA: It's emotional. Parents cry. Kids are excited. Teachers tell us 
attendance goes up—kids feel seen and supported. One boy last year, 
Danny, said it was the first time anyone had asked him about his dreams. 
He wants to be an engineer. We're helping him with math tutoring now.

Kevin and I text almost every day, sharing updates. He's in Toronto 
working—I'm here on the island making the magic happen. It's a true 
diaspora partnership.

What's next?

KEVIN: We want to scale to 500 kids by 2027 and expand to Cariacou 
and Petit Martinique. We're also launching a "diaspora mentor" program 
where Grenadian professionals guide island students into careers.

My advice to others in diaspora: Start small. Start local. See what 
your family needs and meet it. Kevin & Maya's story didn't start as 
this big initiative—it started as one conversation between siblings 
about kids back home.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

- [ ] Q&A or narrative is conversational and personal
- [ ] Support options are clear and varied (donate, volunteer, share, participate)
- [ ] Secondary spotlights represent different initiative types
- [ ] All links tested and working
- [ ] Community directory is current and verified
- [ ] Tone is celebratory, never condescending or "savior" framing
- [ ] Diaspora angle is clear
- [ ] Images are high-quality and properly credited
- [ ] Mobile-friendly layout verified
- [ ] No hardcoded secrets or internal URLs
- [ ] Unsubscribe link visible
